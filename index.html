Subverted Cipher

Algorithm substitution · AES-CTR that leaks its own key · unique ciphertexts

Swap a real AES-CTR encryptor for one that still decrypts correctly and can pass the checks shown here with your own key, watch the holder of a second key read your key out of the ciphertexts, then try the same trick on a scheme with nothing left to choose.

WHY IT MATTERS

The code that encrypts your traffic was written by someone else, and the "random" bytes it emits cannot be audited from outside: an IV that looks random can be a message to whoever planted the code. Bellare, Paterson and Rogaway showed in 2014 that this works against every randomized, stateless scheme in common use, and that the cure is a scheme in which each message has exactly one valid ciphertext.

A teaching demo, not production crypto. The AES, HMAC-SHA-256 and AES-GCM are real (WebCrypto, plus a FIPS 197 AES block written out so the IV can be enciphered and deciphered in the open), and every subversion key lives only in this page's memory for this session. The user-side checks in exhibits 1 and 2 illustrate undetectability; the proof is the paper's and rests on F being a PRF.

Source: M. Bellare, K. G. Paterson, P. Rogaway, "Security of Symmetric Encryption against Mass Surveillance" (CRYPTO 2014, IACR ePrint 2014/438).
